# 🌿 Concept Map — Environmental Governance

This map shows how ideas in my research connect across legal pluralism, polycentric governance, and landscape governance.

It is not fixed. It evolves as my thinking evolves.

---

## 🌍 CORE GOVERNANCE QUESTION

How are landscapes governed when multiple legal systems, institutions, and actors coexist?

↓

---

## ⚖️ 1. LEGAL PLURALISM (Law & Authority)

Focus:
Multiple legal and normative orders coexisting in one space.

Key ideas:
- Authority
- Recognition
- Legitimacy
- Power

Scholars:
- Griffiths
- Tamanaha
- Benda-Beckmann
- Bavinck & Gupta

Question it raises:
→ Who has the authority to define rules?

↓

Connects to:
- How decisions are made
- Who is recognised as legitimate actor

---

## 🏛️ 2. POLYCENTRIC GOVERNANCE (Decision Systems)

Focus:
Multiple decision-making centres interacting.

Key ideas:
- Coordination
- Autonomy
- Decision centres
- Learning

Scholars:
- Elinor Ostrom
- Adaptive governance literature

Question it raises:
→ How do multiple institutions coordinate without hierarchy?

↓

Connects to:
- Fragmented authority
- Multi-level governance
- Institutional interaction

---

## 🌳 3. LANDSCAPE GOVERNANCE (Space & Scale)

Focus:
Governance across spatially connected ecosystems.

Key ideas:
- Scale
- Trade-offs
- Land-use interaction
- Multi-functionality

Scholars:
- Landscape approach literature
- Sayer et al.

Question it raises:
→ How are competing land uses coordinated across space?

↓

Connects to:
- Conservation vs livelihood trade-offs
- Spatial overlap of institutions

---

## 🌱 4. WHERE THEY MEET (MY RESEARCH SPACE)

In Kapuas Hulu, these three dimensions overlap:

- Multiple legal systems (legal pluralism)
- Multiple decision centres (polycentric governance)
- Multiple land uses (landscape governance)

But they do NOT align neatly.

Instead they create tensions:

- Recognition ≠ coordination
- Authority ≠ decision power
- Spatial governance ≠ legal governance

---

## 🌿 5. MY CURRENT SYNTHESIS

Governance in my research is:

> A negotiated system of overlapping legal orders, decision-making centres, and spatial practices.

It is not one system.

It is an interacting landscape of governance.
