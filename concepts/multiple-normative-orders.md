# Beyond Stakeholders: Landscape Governance Through Multiple Normative Orders
## Opening reflection

I view landscape governance as more than a process of coordinating multiple stakeholders or recognised actors. Landscapes are arenas where multiple normative orders coexist and interact, shaping how authority, legitimacy, and practices are negotiated. These normative orders include statutory regulations, customary norms, community-based rules, and intervention-based norms introduced through conservation and development projects.

This perspective highlights why insights from legal pluralism and polycentric governance are complementary for understanding the complexity of landscape governance. While legal pluralism draws attention to the coexistence and interaction of multiple normative orders, polycentric governance helps explain how multiple centres of authority and decision-making interact across scales. Together, these perspectives provide a way to examine how landscape governance emerges through ongoing negotiation among diverse institutions, actors, and sources of legitimacy.
## Connecting ideas

This reflection builds on my interest in how landscape governance operates in contexts where formal institutions, customary arrangements, community organisations, and external interventions overlap.

Key questions emerging from this perspective include:

- How do different normative orders interact within landscape governance arrangements?
- When do overlapping institutions enable collaboration, and when do they create tensions?
- How do external conservation and development interventions reshape local authority and legitimacy?
- How can polycentric governance account for unequal power among different normative orders?
