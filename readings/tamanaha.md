## Where you are in the garden

## Main idea

Tamanaha questions simple understandings of legal pluralism by asking a fundamental question:

What do we mean by "law"?

## Important contribution

Legal pluralism depends on how legal phenomena are identified.

Not every social norm or rule is necessarily law.

The relationship between law, social practices, and institutions needs careful examination.

## Connection to my thinking

This is important for environmental governance because conservation involves many types of rules:

- state regulations
- customary practices
- project requirements
- community norms
- informal agreements

The question is not only whether these rules exist, but how they become recognised as legitimate forms of governance.

## Reflection

Tamanaha encourages caution:

Calling everything "law" may hide important differences between:

- legal authority
- social norms
- institutional practices
- moral expectations
