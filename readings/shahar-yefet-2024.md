Shahar, Y. & Yefet, K. (2024).

## Main idea

Legal pluralism is not only about the existence of multiple legal orders.

It is also about how these legal orders interact, compete, influence, and transform each other.

## Key propositions

### 1. Plurality

Multiple legal orders coexist in the same social space.

Examples:

- State law
- Customary law
- Community rules
- Institutional regulations

### 2. Relationality

Legal orders do not operate separately.

They interact and shape each other.

Question:

How do state conservation rules influence customary governance?

### 3. Power

Different legal orders have unequal authority.

Some are formally recognized.

Others may have legitimacy without official recognition.

Question:

Who has the power to define which rules count?

### 4. Agency

People actively navigate between different legal systems.

Actors may:

- negotiate
- adapt
- combine rules
- strategically use different authorities

## My reflection

This framework helps explain conservation governance because environmental decision-making often involves overlapping authorities.

Legal pluralism is therefore not only a condition of multiple laws, but a process of negotiation among actors.
