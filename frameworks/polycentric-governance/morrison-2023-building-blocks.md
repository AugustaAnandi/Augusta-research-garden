# 🌿 Morrison et al. (2023): Building Blocks of Polycentric Governance

## Why this paper matters

As research on polycentric governance has expanded, different studies have emphasised different aspects of governance systems.

Morrison et al propose a common analytical framework—a set of "building blocks" to better understand how polycentric governance is organised and how it functions in practice.

For my research, this paper is valuable because it shifts attention from defining polycentric governance to analysing how governance systems actually operate.

---

## The central question

How can polycentric governance be analysed in a consistent and systematic way?

---

## Core contribution

The paper identifies key components of polycentric governance and shows how these components interact.

Rather than viewing polycentric governance as simply the existence of multiple actors, the framework examines how institutions, actors, policy issues, and decision-making arenas are connected.

---

## Key ideas I take from this paper

### Governance is relational

Polycentric governance is defined by interactions rather than isolated organisations.

### Institutions are interconnected

Understanding governance requires examining how organisations coordinate, exchange information, negotiate, and learn.

### Governance evolves

Relationships among actors change over time in response to new environmental, political, and social conditions.

### Coordination is observable

Polycentric governance can be studied through patterns of collaboration, interaction, and institutional linkages.

---

## How I use this paper in my research

This framework helps me think systematically about governance in Kapuas Hulu.

Rather than asking only **who participates**, I can also ask:

- How are actors connected?
- Which organisations coordinate?
- Where are governance gaps?
- Which relationships are strong or weak?

---

## Where I see its limitations

The framework provides a useful way to analyse institutional relationships.

However, understanding these relationships also requires attention to legal recognition, authority, and power.

For this reason, I combine this framework with legal pluralism.
