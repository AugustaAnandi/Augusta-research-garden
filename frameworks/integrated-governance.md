# Integrated Governance in My Research

## Why this page exists

This page brings together three frameworks that I use to understand environmental governance in my research:

- Legal Pluralism
- Polycentric Governance
- Landscape Governance

They are often treated separately in the literature, but in my case study (Kapuas Hulu), they appear together in practice.

---

## 1. Legal Pluralism → Authority and Recognition

Legal pluralism helps me understand that multiple normative systems coexist in the same landscape:

- State law (protected areas, village forest regulations)
- Customary systems (Dayak and Malay practices)
- Project-based rules (NGO, conservation programs)
- Informal norms and agreements

### Key contribution to my research

It helps explain **how authority is constructed and recognised**, not only how rules exist.

---

## 2. Polycentric Governance → Coordination and Decision Centres

Polycentric governance helps me understand that there are multiple centres of decision-making:

- Village institutions
- Forest management units (KPH)
- National park authorities
- NGOs and conservation organisations
- District and provincial government

### Key contribution to my research

It helps explain **how decisions are made across multiple institutions**, not in a single hierarchy.

---

## 3. Landscape Governance → Spatial Integration

Landscape governance helps me understand that governance happens across space and sectors:

- Forests and protected areas
- Agricultural land
- Fisheries and river systems
- Oil palm and conservation boundaries

### Key contribution to my research

It helps explain **how different land uses and actors interact across a shared landscape**.

---

## 4. How these three frameworks interact

In my research, these frameworks are not separate layers.

They describe different dimensions of the same reality:

- Legal Pluralism → focuses on **normative orders and authority**
- Polycentric Governance → focuses on **decision-making structures**
- Landscape Governance → focuses on **spatial and sectoral interactions**

Together, they help explain governance in Kapuas Hulu as:

> a landscape with multiple legal systems, multiple decision centres, and multiple land-use logics interacting simultaneously.

---

## 5. My current synthesis

In Kapuas Hulu, governance is not only:

- plural (multiple legal systems)
- polycentric (multiple institutions)
- or landscape-based (multiple spatial uses)

It is all three at once.

However, these dimensions do not align neatly:

- Legal systems are not equally recognised
- Decision centres are not equally powerful
- Spatial governance is shaped by unequal authority

---

## 6. Emerging question

How do unequal legal orders, decision centres, and spatial claims interact in a single governance landscape?
