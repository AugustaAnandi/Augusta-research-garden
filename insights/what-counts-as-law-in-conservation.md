# What counts as law in conservation?

## Starting question

When does a social practice become recognised as a legal order?

## Thinking through the literature

Tamanaha reminds us to be careful about defining everything as law.

Griffiths shows that law cannot only be understood through state institutions.

Benda-Beckmann shows that legal orders operate through social relationships and practice.

## My emerging view

In conservation landscapes, the question is not only whether customary rules are "law."

The more important question is:

How do different actors recognise, negotiate, and use different sources of authority?

## Example

A community may have customary rules about forest use.

However, when entering a conservation partnership, those rules may interact with:

- government regulations
- conservation agreements
- donor requirements

The result is not simply replacement of one system by another.

It is negotiation between different forms of authority.
